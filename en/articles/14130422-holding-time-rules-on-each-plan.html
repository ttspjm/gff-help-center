Holding time rules on each plan

Updated 9 November 2026

Since Monday 9 November 2026, every GFF plan uses one holding time rule: the 1 minute rule. The 2 minute microscalping rule no longer applies on any plan.

The 1 minute rule

At each payout request, both of the following must be true for that payout cycle:

  • at least 70% of your closed trades were held longer than 1 minute, and
  • at least 70% of your net profit came from trades held longer than 1 minute.

If either is not met, the payout request is declined and the profit from trades held under 1 minute in that cycle is deducted from the account. This is not an account breach, and your account stays open.

The same check applies when you pass an evaluation. A partial close counts as its own closed trade, even if the rest of the position is still running.

Which plans it applies to

All of them: EOD Challenge, 1-Day-Pass-Plan, Flex Challenge, Daily Payouts, Instant Classic, Instant Lite and GOAT Access. It applies to new and existing accounts.

It does not apply on live accounts after the transition. There, we ask you to trade with the care of managing actual live capital.

What changed on 9 November

  • EOD Challenge, 1-Day-Pass-Plan, Instant Classic and Instant Lite: profit from trades closed within 2 minutes is no longer deducted trade by trade. The 1 minute rule is checked at payout instead.
  • Flex Challenge: the threshold rises from 50% to 70%.
  • Daily Payouts: the 60 second rule becomes the 1 minute rule. It now checks both your closed trades and your net profit, at 70%.
  • GOAT Access: uses the same 1 minute rule as every plan.

Trading and payout cycles up to Sunday 8 November follow the previous rules, including the 2 minute microscalping deduction.

Tick scalping

Opening and closing positions within seconds to capture small tick profits, typically $10 to $50 a time, is not permitted on any account. Profit generated this way may be reviewed and deducted.

Why this rule exists

So that results reflect what would realistically be executable on a live exchange, where slippage, partial fills and liquidity limits apply.

It should not be confused with the 2 minute news buffer, which is a separate restriction around high impact news events and has not changed.
